Golf Trips to Ireland

The best collection of links golf in the world, an hour's flight from almost anywhere in the UK.

GolfTrip › Destinations › Ireland Golf Trips

Ireland's case is simple: nowhere else has this many great links courses this close together, and none of them are far from a UK airport. Ballybunion, Waterville and Tralee in the south west. Portmarnock, The Island and Baltray around Dublin. Royal County Down and Royal Portrush on the Causeway Coast, forty minutes apart and both routinely in the world's top ten. Lahinch and Doonbeg in Clare. Enniscrone, Carne and Rosapenna in the west, where the dunes are enormous and the tee sheet is empty.

What has changed is the price. Green fees at the top end have risen hard for 2026 and again for 2027 — Lahinch went up 20% in a year, Royal County Down sold its 2027 visitor allocation in seven hours. The premium rounds are now a genuine budget decision rather than an assumption. The good news is the second tier is superb and a fraction of the cost, and a well-built itinerary mixes the two.

Where to Play

The Courses Worth Building a Trip Around

Kerry

Ballybunion Old

The one Tom Watson called the finest seaside course he'd seen. A caddie or fore-caddie is required with every group, and there's a handicap limit of 24 for men, 36 for ladies.

Green fee: €400 (caddie €70–€160 extra)

Kerry

Waterville

The end of the Ring of Kerry, and the back nine along the dunes is as good as links golf gets. Caddies around €100, not guaranteed — book them.

Green fee: €400–€425

Kerry

Tralee

Arnold Palmer's first European design, on a stretch of coast used in Ryan's Daughter. The shoulder-season rate is one of the best deals in Irish golf.

Green fee: €250 shoulder / €450 high season

Clare

Lahinch Old

Old Tom Morris and Alister MacKenzie, and the famous blind par-3 Dell. Up 20% for 2026, which tells you how much demand there is.

Green fee: €450

Clare

Trump Doonbeg

Enormous dunes and a resort attached, with the widest seasonal price range on this list — the low-season rate is under half the summer weekend one.

Green fee: €250–€525 by season and day

Dublin

Portmarnock

The classic Dublin members' links and one of the great flat-out tests of ball-striking. Buggies need a medical certificate. Lunch included in the fee.

Green fee: Around €530 including lunch

Dublin area

County Louth (Baltray)

An hour north of the city and a genuine connoisseur's course — less famous than Portmarnock, no less good, considerably cheaper.

Green fee: €365

Co. Down

Royal County Down

Regularly ranked the best course in the world. Championship links on select weekdays, April to October; the tee sheet opens once a year and vanishes.

Green fee: £450 (caddie £75–£110, cash)

Co. Antrim

Royal Portrush (Dunluce)

2025 Open venue, and busier than ever because of it. The Valley Links next door is a fine course in its own right at less than half the price.

Green fee: £420 (Valley £200)

Co. Londonderry

Portstewart Strand

The front nine through the dunes is worth the trip on its own. The winter rate on mats is the cheapest serious links golf in Ireland.

Green fee: £70 winter / £195 shoulder / £335 summer

The west — Enniscrone, Carne at Belmullet, Rosapenna and Ballyliffin — is where the value is, with big dune golf and quiet tee sheets. Rates there move around and we couldn't confirm current figures for all of them, so ring the club; you'll usually be pleasantly surprised.

On the prices: these are indicative 2026 visitor rates gathered from course and operator listings, shown to help you budget. Green fees change with season, day of the week and where you're staying — always confirm direct with the course before you commit a group to it.

Direct fromDublin: twenty-plus UK airports. Kerry: Luton, Stansted, Manchester. Shannon: Heathrow, Stansted, Manchester and more. Belfast: most of the UK

By ferryHolyhead–Dublin is about 3h15; Cairnryan–Belfast about 2h15. Worth it for a group driving with clubs and trolleys — car and passengers typically £125–£400 depending on sailing
Driving between regionsDublin to Killarney about 3h30; Dublin to Portrush about 3h. Don't try to combine Kerry and the Causeway Coast in one short trip

Where to Stay

The Bases Groups Actually Use

Killarney

The south west base. Ballybunion, Waterville, Tralee and Dooks all reachable, plus enough pubs and restaurants to keep a society occupied for a week.

Portrush & Portstewart

The Causeway Coast. Royal Portrush, Portstewart and Castlerock on the doorstep and Royal County Down about ninety minutes away. Sterling, so easy for a UK group.

Dublin, Malahide & Portmarnock

For Portmarnock, The Island and Baltray, with a city on the doorstep. The easiest Irish trip to organise and the best for non-golfing evenings.

Lahinch or Doonbeg

Clare. Small, golf-obsessed towns where the course is the centre of things. Shannon is under an hour away.

Before You Book

Worth Knowing

  • Royal County Down and Royal Portrush need booking about twelve months out. RCD releases its visitor tee sheet once a year, usually late March or early April for the following season, first come first served — the 2027 allocation went in seven hours. If these two are the reason for the trip, work backwards from that release date.
  • Caddies are part of the deal, not an upsell. Ballybunion requires a caddie or fore-caddie with every group. Waterville and Royal County Down expect one. Budget roughly £75–€200 per caddie per round, usually cash, with the tip on top.
  • Two currencies on one trip. Euro south of the border, sterling north of it. Whoever's collecting the money should decide early which currency the trip is priced in, or the spreadsheet becomes a running argument.
  • The weather is the weather. No month in Ireland is reliably dry — even June averages around 80mm of rain. Full waterproofs, spare gloves and a second pair of shoes per person. Groups who pack for it have a great time; groups who don't spend the week in the clubhouse.
