Golf Trips to Scotland

Every golfer should do this trip once. It costs more than you think and it's worth more than it costs.

GolfTrip › Destinations › Scotland Golf Trips

Scotland is the one destination where the golf is the entire point. Nobody flies to Fife in April for the weather. They go because the Old Course, Carnoustie, North Berwick, Muirfield and Royal Troon exist within a couple of hours of each other, because you walk them, and because a links round in a crosswind is a different game from the one you play at home.

It is also the trip that needs the most planning. Green fees at the marquee venues have risen sharply — the Old Course is £355 and Kingsbarns is £486 for 2026 — Muirfield takes visitors on two days a week and is effectively full for the season, and the Old Course cannot simply be booked. Get the admin right twelve months out and it's the best golf trip in the world. Leave it to March and you'll be playing the Eden.

Where to Play

The Courses Worth Building a Trip Around

Fife

Old Course, St Andrews

The one. Closed Sundays, walking only, maximum handicap 24 for men and 36 for ladies, and you cannot just book it — see the ballot section below.

Green fee: £355

Fife

Kingsbarns

Twenty-five years old and already on every top-100 list. Sea views from every hole, and the most expensive round in Scotland outside Turnberry.

Green fee: £486

Angus

Carnoustie Championship

"Car-nasty." The hardest of the Open rota courses and the one that will most accurately tell your group what their handicaps are worth.

Green fee: £360

East Lothian

North Berwick West Links

1832, quirky in the best way, stone walls and blind shots. Most golfers who play it come off saying it was their favourite round of the trip.

Green fee: £320

East Lothian

Gullane No. 1

Up over Gullane Hill with a view across the Firth of Forth to Fife. Three courses on site, so a society can play No. 1 and No. 2 on the same day.

Green fee: £325

East Lothian

Muirfield

Visitors on Tuesdays and Thursdays only, and 2026 is essentially full with a waiting list. Enquire twelve months out or don't count on it.

Green fee: £395 (£605 for 36 with lunch)

Ayrshire

Royal Troon Old

2024 Open venue, unforgiving back nine into the prevailing wind. Sources differ slightly on the 2026 rate — confirm with the club.

Green fee: Around £365–£395

Ayrshire

Trump Turnberry (Ailsa)

Arguably the most beautiful links in Britain, priced to keep the tee sheet for resort guests. Play after 1pm or off-peak and it halves.

Green fee: Up to £1,000 peak; £545 after 1pm; from £315 off-peak

Ayrshire

Dundonald Links

The value round on the Ayrshire coast, with a proper seasonal price ladder and a discount if you're staying on site.

Green fee: £105 winter to £245–£260 peak

East Lothian

Craigielaw

Modern links, friendly to a mixed society, and the cheapest good round in East Lothian. Caddies around £60 plus gratuity.

Green fee: £80–£160 by season and day

The other St Andrews Links courses — New, Jubilee, Castle, Eden and Strathtyrum — sit well below the Old Course and are the sensible way to fill an itinerary. We haven't quoted their 2026 fees because the figures we could find were out of date; check standrews.com.

On the prices: these are indicative 2026 visitor rates gathered from course and operator listings, shown to help you budget. Green fees change with season, day of the week and where you're staying — always confirm direct with the course before you commit a group to it.

May–June14–17°CThe sweet spot. Statistically drier than July and August, and around seventeen hours of daylight in June means 36 holes a day is genuinely possible.
July–August16–20°CMild but wetter than late spring, and peak green fees everywhere. Busiest tee sheets of the year.

Where to Stay

The Bases Groups Actually Use

St Andrews town

Walk to the Old, New and Jubilee. Everything from a Premier Inn to the Old Course Hotel and Rusacks. Carnoustie and Kingsbarns are day trips from here.

North Berwick or Gullane

East Lothian. Both villages put you on foot from their own links and within fifteen minutes of Muirfield and Craigielaw. Edinburgh is 40 minutes away if the group wants a night out.

Troon or Prestwick

Ayrshire. Royal Troon, Prestwick, Western Gailes and Dundonald all within a short drive, and town-hotel prices well below St Andrews for the same quality.

Turnberry resort

A single-base luxury trip. Expensive, extraordinary, and the group never has to get in a car.

Before You Book

Worth Knowing

  • The Old Course cannot simply be booked, and this is the single thing that derails Scotland trips. There are three routes: the annual advance reservation ballot, which opens around September for the following year and requires you to also book a round on the New or Jubilee; the daily ballot, entered two days ahead by 2pm as a group of two to four with names and handicaps, results out around 4:30pm; or an Authorised Provider package booked eighteen to twenty-four months out. Singles cannot enter the main ballot and have to go through the starter or the separate single-golfer route in person. Check the current windows on standrews.com — and note that demand for 2027 is unusually high because The Open returns to St Andrews.
  • Caddies at the Old Course run about £55 plus a £20–£25 gratuity. Not compulsory, but on a course where you genuinely cannot see where you're aiming, the best £80 of the trip. Buggies are not permitted; trolleys only after midday in season.
  • Collect everyone's handicap before you travel. The Old Course caps at 24 for men, and several clubs ask for proof at booking and again on the tee. A WHS app screenshot is normally accepted; a club certificate never gets argued with.
  • These are walking courses in weather that changes hourly. Proper waterproofs, spare gloves, a towel and two pairs of shoes are not optional extras. Members will tell you there's no such thing as bad weather, only bad clothing, and they are annoyingly right.
