Golf Trips to Spain

More golf per square mile than anywhere else in Europe, and the season runs when yours at home doesn't.

GolfTrip › Destinations › Spain Golf Trips

The Costa del Sol has around seventy courses inside an hour's drive, direct flights from more than twenty UK airports, and — the part that catches British golfers out — its golf high season is winter, not summer. While your home course is waterlogged in January, Marbella is playing firm in sixteen degrees and the tee sheets are full of Scandinavians and Scots.

Two hours up the coast, the Costa Blanca around Alicante does the same job for noticeably less money, with Las Colinas and the Villamartin cluster leading a set of courses that suit a society trip better than a bucket-list one. And if the group has the budget for one properly special round, Sotogrande has Valderrama, Real Club de Golf Sotogrande and La Reserva within a few minutes of each other — a concentration of serious golf that has no equal in Spain.

Where to Play

The Courses Worth Building a Trip Around

Sotogrande

Valderrama

The 1997 Ryder Cup course and still the most demanding tee-to-green test in Spain. A forecaddie is mandatory, not optional, and paid in cash on the day.

Green fee: Around €600 + forecaddie

Sotogrande

Real Club de Golf Sotogrande

Trent Jones Snr, 1964, and the course a lot of good judges prefer to its famous neighbour. Access is restricted — usually via a hotel package or an operator.

Green fee: €385 weekday / €420 weekend

Sotogrande

La Reserva Club

Modern, immaculate and priced per pair with a shared buggy. The most forgiving of the three Sotogrande heavyweights for a mixed group.

Green fee: €560–€750 per pair with buggy

Nueva Andalucía, Marbella

Los Naranjos

Trent Jones again, in the middle of Marbella's Golf Valley. Buggy included in the fee, and a genuinely good test that won't ruin the weaker players.

Green fee: €139–€177 with buggy

Mijas Costa

La Cala Resort

Three full courses on one site — America, Asia and Europa. The three-course pass is the best value multi-round deal on the coast.

Green fee: €231–€333 for all three rounds

Costa Blanca

Las Colinas

Consistently rated the best course on the Costa Blanca and about a third of the price of the Sotogrande names. Half an hour from Alicante.

Green fee: €98–€134

Casares

Finca Cortesín

Solheim Cup 2023 host and one of the finest resort courses in Europe. It doesn't publish a rate card — you enquire, and you pay accordingly.

Green fee: On enquiry

Also on most society itineraries: Villaitana's Levante and Poniente courses near Benidorm, Villamartin and the Orihuela Costa cluster, Santa Clara and Marbella Golf, and Alcaidesa's links course looking across at Gibraltar.

Timing

When to Go

MonthsTypical daytimeWhat to expect
November–February12–20°CThe Costa del Sol's golf high season, and the reason UK clubs come. Dry, mild, sunny; tee sheets and green fees are at their busiest and dearest.
March–May18–25°CThe best overall balance — warm enough to enjoy, cheaper than midwinter, courses in excellent condition after the winter rain.
June–August28–33°CGolf low season. Too hot for a comfortable afternoon 18, so green fees drop hard. Fine if you'll take a 7:30am tee time and mean it.
September–October24–29°CEffectively an extension of summer with better playing temperatures. Busy again by October as the winter crowd arrives.

Where to Stay

The Bases Groups Actually Use

Marbella & Puerto Banús

The obvious base. Golf Valley on the doorstep, every restaurant and bar the group could want, and prices to match on the Banús side.

Mijas Costa & Fuengirola

Better value, La Cala's three courses close by, and an easy 30-minute run from Málaga. The pragmatic society choice.

Estepona, Benahavís & Sotogrande

Quieter and more upmarket. Where you stay if Valderrama, Sotogrande or Finca Cortesín are the point of the trip.

Orihuela Costa & Alicante

Costa Blanca. Las Colinas, Villamartin and Campoamor within fifteen minutes, and the cheapest good golf in mainland Spain.

Before You Book

Worth Knowing

  • Winter is peak, summer is cheap. If your society is used to pricing Spain like a beach holiday you will get this backwards. February costs more than July for exactly the same golf.
  • Valderrama's forecaddie is compulsory and cash. Around €60–€70 per group on the day. Tell the group in advance so nobody arrives at Sotogrande without euros in their pocket.
  • Handicap certificates are rarely checked at resort courses, sometimes at member clubs. The exclusive Sotogrande clubs do ask. Flag any high handicappers to the operator when you book — slow play is what these clubs actually care about.
  • Book the marquee names nine to twelve months out. Group tee times at peak times on the best courses go a long way ahead. Everything else on the coast can be arranged with a few weeks' notice.
